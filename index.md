# Thumbnail Testing Notes for a Small YouTube Channel

A channel with a few thousand subscribers cannot run statistically clean A/B tests, but it can still learn what works. These are the habits I settled on after a year of uploading weekly.

## Make three, not one
For every video I sketch three thumbnail directions: a face with a clear expression, an object close-up, and a before/after split. Producing three takes about twenty minutes once the template is ready.

## Generate backgrounds, not text
Backgrounds and props can come from an image generator. I use [Seedream 5.5](https://seedream5-5.net/) for backdrops such as "cluttered workbench, shallow depth of field, empty right half", then place the title text and my own photo in an editor so the lettering stays sharp.

## Read it at 120 pixels
Most views come from the home feed and suggested sidebar, where thumbnails are tiny. If the main idea is not visible at 120 pixels wide, it does not work.

## Swap after 48 hours
If click-through is clearly below the channel average after two days, swap in the second option. Keep a spreadsheet: video, variant, CTR before, CTR after.

## What I learned
- High-contrast single subjects beat busy collages.
- Three words or fewer on the image.
- Consistent colors make the channel recognisable in a feed.
